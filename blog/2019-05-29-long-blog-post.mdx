Documentation helps me slow down enough to understand what I am actually building.

{/* truncate */}

When a project is moving fast, it is easy to keep all the context in my head: why I chose a part, which test failed, what I changed in the design, or where a useful reference came from. A few weeks later, that context becomes harder to recover.

Writing the process down gives each project a memory. It also makes the work more useful to others. A finished PCB, a 3D printed part, or a working firmware demo is helpful, but the path to that result is often where the real learning is.

For each project, I try to capture:

- the idea and motivation
- the materials, tools, and references used
- design files, code, and fabrication steps
- mistakes, debugging notes, and test results
- what I would improve in the next version

Not every post will be polished. Some notes will be rough, because rough notes are still better than losing the details completely.
